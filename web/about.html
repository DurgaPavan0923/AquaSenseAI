Dataset Origin & Labeling Rule Audit

Dataset Source: Kaggle Water Quality & Pollution Monitoring Dataset (1,000 observations, 8 numerical features, 1 target label).

Investigation of Target Construction: Statistical audit reveals that the target risk classifications (Good: 559, Moderate: 198, Polluted: 243) were generated using composite multi-parameter threshold criteria based on standard water quality index guidelines (e.g. Dissolved Oxygen < 5.2 mg/L, BOD > 4.5 mg/L, Turbidity > 8.0 NTU, and elevated heavy metal concentrations define Polluted samples).

Scientific Research Limitation Notice: Because the ground-truth labels in this dataset are rule-derived from the input parameters, the machine learning models learn to approximate and reproduce these composite labeling rules with high mathematical fidelity (Macro-F1: 99.43%), rather than discovering novel unmeasured biochemical contaminants. The model functions as a rapid, automated screening triage tool, and outputs must not be interpreted as certified laboratory validation.

Data Leakage Prevention: The dataset contains 0 duplicate feature rows. All preprocessors (StandardScaler, DomainFeatureEngineer) are fit exclusively on the training partition. Four pairwise disjoint partitions ensure zero contamination.

4-Way Partition Methodology

To ensure strict exchangeability and prevent overoptimistic conformal calibration, the observations are partitioned into four distinct splits:

Partition Proportion Sample Size Methodological Purpose
Base Model Training 50% 500 samples Fits feature scaling, domain ratio calculations, and tree structures.
Probability Calibration 15% 150 samples Calibrates sigmoid Platt scaling probabilities using pre-fit CV splits.
Conformal Calibration 15% 150 samples Derives non-conformity threshold q_hat without contamination from model fitting.
Holdout Test Partition 20% 200 samples Independent final validation of empirical coverage and classification metrics.

Authoritative Water Quality Standards

Parameters are evaluated against authoritative criteria from the World Health Organization (WHO), the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS IS 10500:2012), and the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB):

Parameter Applicable Domain Statutory Limit Authoritative Standard Scientific Rationale
pH Drinking & Surface Water 6.50 to 8.50 WHO 4th Ed. / BIS IS 10500:2012 pH < 6.5 accelerates corrosion; pH > 8.5 impedes chlorination efficiency.
Turbidity Drinking Water ≤ 5.0 NTU WHO Guidelines / BIS IS 10500:2012 Colloidal suspension shields pathogenic microorganisms from disinfection.
Dissolved Oxygen Surface Water & Ecology ≥ 6.50 mg/L CPCB Class A / EPA Freshwater Criteria Critical for aquatic organism respiration and biological oxidation of organic wastes.
BOD (5-Day) Surface Water Quality ≤ 2.00 mg/L CPCB Class A Drinking Source Benchmark High biochemical oxygen demand indicates organic waste and sewage contamination.
Lead (Pb) Drinking Water Toxicant ≤ 0.010 mg/L WHO Guideline / BIS IS 10500:2012 Cumulative neurotoxin causing neurological and renal damage upon chronic exposure.
Mercury (Hg) Drinking Water Toxicant ≤ 0.001 mg/L BIS IS 10500:2012 / US EPA MCL Severe nephrotoxin and neurotoxin with bioaccumulative organic pathways.
Arsenic (As) Drinking Water Toxicant ≤ 0.010 mg/L WHO Provisional / BIS IS 10500:2012 Group 1 human carcinogen associated with skin lesions and systemic cancers.

Responsible Use & Screening Protocol

AquaSense AI provides upfront rapid screening support to triage municipal and field samples. It does not replace certified laboratory testing. The following restrictions apply:

  • Microbiological pathogens (Escherichia coli, Cryptosporidium, Giardia) are not measured directly and require certified membrane filtration laboratory incubation.
  • Unmonitored toxic chemicals (pesticides, PFAS, radionuclides) require mass spectrometry (GC-MS / LC-MS) analysis.
  • Any sample flagged with "Needs laboratory test" or an anomalous sensor reading must be routed to an accredited environmental testing facility prior to municipal or potable distribution.
